\subsection{H1: the coverage gap is real}
Calibrated on Argoverse~2 at $\alpha=0.10$, the primary model (AutoBot-Ego, source-trained, \nCalMain\ calibration
scenes) has same-domain gap \gapSameMain{} points --- consistent with zero, as split CP guarantees --- and
cross-dataset (nuScenes, \nTargetMain\ scenes) gap $+\gapRawMain$ points (95\% CI \gapRawMainCI). This replicates the
preliminary Run~1 measurement (+3.4pt) on an independent calibration/test split. Table~\ref{tab:models} shows a second,
more heavily trained model with a substantially \emph{larger} gap ($+8.0$pt) in the same direction: the failure is
reproducible, but its magnitude is not a fixed property of the dataset pair --- it depends on the source model. We
report this without averaging it away.

\subsection{H2/H3: covariate reweighting does not repair it}
Weighting the calibration scores by an estimated label-free covariate-shift likelihood ratio does not close the gap;
it widens it. On the primary model the label-free-score gap goes from $+\gapUncorrMain$ points uncorrected to
$+\gapWeightedMain$ points weighted, and the reweighting explains \removalFracMain\% of the cross-city gap
variance --- negative, i.e.\ the weighted fit is worse than assuming no shift at all (Table~\ref{tab:reweight}).
This is consistent across both models analysed so far and matches the Run~1 finding: it is evidence for conditional,
not purely covariate, shift, and is the empirical motivation for Proposition~\ref{prop:ident} (no label-free method
can be expected to repair an unrestricted shift).

\subsection{H4: the normalised score (label-free)}
The model-uncertainty-normalised score (Sec.~\ref{sec:norm}) reduces the primary model's gap by \normReductionMain\%
(from $+\gapRawMain$ to $+\gapNormMain$ points). Table~\ref{tab:models}'s ``red.'' column shows this is not a
uniform effect: on the second model the same normalisation reduces the gap by only 12\%. H4 is therefore \emph{not}
confirmed as a general repair --- it helps substantially on one model and barely on another, and we report both
rather than the more favourable one.

\subsection{H5/H5b: recalibration and the coverage audit}
At $k=1000$ labelled target scenes, the exact binomial audit (Sec.~\ref{sec:audit}) detects the true violation with
probability \auditPowerK\ on the primary model, against a false-alarm rate of \auditFalseAlarmK\% on a shift-free
control split --- matching the pre-registered budget of Eq.~\eqref{eq:budget} ($k\approx750$ for the analogous
$\delta=0.034$ case) to the same order of magnitude. \todo{Add label-budget-curve figure (direct/pooled/shrinkage
estimators vs.\ $k$) once finalised; report $k^\star$ per model.}

\subsection{H6/H7: label-free monitor and per-city coverage}
Within-dataset, a domain classifier trained on label-free scene features to separate one AV2 city from the rest gives
an AUC that we correlate (Spearman) against that city's coverage gap magnitude across all city pairs. On the primary
model $\rho=0.37$ ($p=0.04$); on the second model $\rho=0.10$ ($p=0.61$, not significant). The pre-registered
threshold was $\rho\ge0.5$: \textbf{H6 is not confirmed} on either model. We report this as a negative result --- the
label-free monitor tracks shift only weakly, and not reliably enough to substitute for the labelled audit of H5b.
