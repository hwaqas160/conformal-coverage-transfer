\section{Methods}
\subsection{Model-normalised score (label-free)}\label{sec:norm}
The forecaster emits, per mode $k$, a Laplace scale $(b^x_{k,t},b^y_{k,t})$. Let
$u_k(x)=\tfrac1T\sum_t\sqrt{(b^x_{k,t})^2+(b^y_{k,t})^2}$ and use the score
$s'(x,y)=\min_k \max_t\|\hat y_{k,t}-y_t\|/u_k(x)$. The region is the union of balls of radius $\hat q'\,u_k(x)$. The premise is that when a
shift makes scenes harder, the forecaster's own scale widens, so the normalised score is less shift-sensitive. Its
efficiency is measured by region area $\sum_k\pi(\hat q' u_k)^2$ at matched coverage.

\subsection{Coverage audit}\label{sec:audit}
Given a source-calibrated $\hat q$ and $k$ labelled target scenes, an
exact one-sided binomial test of $H_0$: coverage $\ge1-\alpha$ flags
a violation. We report its empirical power and its false-alarm
rate on a shift-free split, alongside the theoretical budget of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:budget}.

\subsection{Recalibration with $k$ target labels}
We compare three thresholds computed from $k$ labelled target scenes and $n$ source scenes:
\emph{direct} (SCP on the $k$ target scores); \emph{pooled} (SCP on the union); and \emph{shrinkage}
($\hat q=\lambda\hat q_{\text{tgt}}+(1-\lambda)\hat q_{\text{src}}$ with $\lambda=k/(k+k_0)$). We report the label budget $k^\star$ at which
absolute coverage error falls below $0.02$ in at least 90\% of random label draws.

\subsection{Certify-or-recalibrate with exchangeable labels}\label{sec:cor}
Plain recalibration on $k$ target labels is only \emph{marginally} valid: the coverage of the resulting threshold is
$\mathrm{Beta}(\ell,k+1-\ell)$-distributed \cite{vovk2012conditional}, so it falls below $1-\alpha$ in roughly half of all
label draws (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}). A practitioner needs the stronger, \emph{training-conditional}
statement \cite{vovk2012conditional,bian2023training}: coverage $\ge 1-\alpha$ with probability $\ge 1-\delta$ over the draw of the labels.
\emph{Certify-or-recalibrate} (C-or-R) delivers it while keeping the source threshold when it can be certified.
With $k$ labelled scores $s_1,\dots,s_k$ and $M=\#\{s_i>\hat q_{\mathrm{src}}\}$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Certify.} Keep $\hat q_{\mathrm{src}}$ iff the one-sided Clopper--Pearson upper confidence bound
\cite{clopper1934confidence} on its miss probability at level $\delta/2$ is $\le\alpha$.
\item \emph{Otherwise recalibrate.} Return the $\ell$-th smallest labelled score, with $\ell$ the smallest integer such that
$\Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(k,1-\alpha)\le \ell-1]\ge 1-\delta/2$ (the $\ell$-th order statistic has coverage $\mathrm{Beta}(\ell,k+1-\ell)$); if no such $\ell\le k$ exists
return $+\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
\begin{proposition}[C-or-R is training-conditionally valid]\label{prop:cor}
If the $k$ target scores are exchangeable with the target scores at test time and continuously distributed, the returned threshold
$\hat q$ satisfies $\Pr\big[\Pr_{\text{test}}(s\le\hat q\mid \text{labels})\ge 1-\alpha\big]\ge 1-\delta$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $F$ be the target score CDF. The event ``$\hat q$ fails'' is contained in (a) the source threshold is certified although
$F(\hat q_{\mathrm{src}})<1-\alpha$, or (b) the branch-2 order statistic has coverage $<1-\alpha$. (a) has probability $\le\delta/2$ because the
Clopper--Pearson bound is a valid one-sided confidence bound for the miss probability $1-F(\hat q_{\mathrm{src}})$ of the fixed threshold; (b) has probability
$\le\delta/2$ by the definition of $\ell$. A union bound gives $\delta$.
\end{proof}
Certification needs slack: a source threshold calibrated at exactly $\alpha$ has miss probability $\approx\alpha$ and its confidence bound cannot fall below
$\alpha$; in our experiments it is (almost) never certified on shifted data, and on unshifted data it is certified only if calibrated at a smaller
$\alpha_{\mathrm{src}}<\alpha$.

\subsection{Scene-level risk control under clustered labels}\label{sec:scene}
Driving datasets label many agents per scene, and agents in one scene share road, traffic and annotator. Labels drawn scene by scene are not
exchangeable at the agent level, and Proposition~\ref{prop:cor} then fails (Sec.~\ref{sec:results}). We therefore make the \emph{scene} the unit.
For a labelled scene $j$ with agent scores $\{s_{ji}\}$ let $L_j(q)=\frac1{n_j}\sum_i\mathbf 1[s_{ji}>q]\in[0,1]$, non-increasing in $q$, and
control the scene-averaged miscoverage $R(q)=\mathbb E_j\,L_j(q)$. Given $m$ labelled scenes, scene-level certify-or-recalibrate
proceeds as in Sec.~\ref{sec:cor} but with Learn-then-Test \cite{angelopoulos2025ltt} over a \emph{fixed}, label-independent grid
$q_1>q_2>\dots>q_G$ (log-spaced around the source threshold, $\hat q_{\mathrm{src}}\times[4,0.5]$, $G=300$): for each candidate we compute a $p$-value for
$H_{q}:R(q)>\alpha$ from the $m$ per-scene losses, test candidates from the largest downwards at level $\delta/2$, and return the last $q$ rejected
($+\infty$ if none). The source threshold is tried first at level $\delta/2$ (certification). Two $p$-values are used:
Hoeffding--Bentkus \cite{bates2021rcps}, and a betting $p$-value \cite{waudbysmith2024betting}: with predictable bets $\lambda_i\in[0,\tfrac1{2\alpha}]$ (variance-adaptive)
the capital $K=\prod_i\big(1+\lambda_i(\alpha-L_i)\big)$ is a non-negative supermartingale under $\mathbb E L\ge\alpha$, so $p=\min(1,1/\max_tK_t)$
is valid by Ville's inequality; being variance-adaptive it exploits that per-scene miss fractions are mostly close to zero.
\begin{proposition}[Scene-level guarantee]\label{prop:scene}
If the $m$ labelled scenes are exchangeable draws from the target scene population, and each $p$-value is valid for its null, the returned
threshold satisfies $\Pr[R(\hat q)\le\alpha]\ge 1-\delta$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$R$ is non-increasing, so along the descending grid the false nulls ($R(q_i)\le\alpha$) form a prefix and the true nulls a suffix beginning at some $i_0$.
The procedure returns a threshold with $R>\alpha$ only if it rejects $H_{q_{i_0}}$ (fixed-sequence testing stops at the first non-rejection), which has probability $\le\delta/2$
because the grid does not depend on the labels; the certification branch fails with probability $\le\delta/2$. A union bound gives $\delta$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Online baseline}
Adaptive conformal inference (ACI) \cite{gibbs2021adaptive} updates the working level $\alpha_{t+1}=\alpha_t+\gamma(\alpha-\mathrm{err}_t)$ after every revealed label, keeping the source
calibration set fixed; it needs a stream of labels and offers long-run, not finite-sample, coverage. We report (i) how many streamed labels ACI needs before its
running coverage stays within $2$ points of nominal, and (ii) the coverage of its threshold frozen after $k$ labels, for $\gamma\in\{0.005,0.01,0.05\}$.

\subsection{Controlled input shifts}\label{sec:inject}
To locate the source of the loss we perturb one measured AV2--nuScenes difference at a time in AV2 test \emph{inputs} (ground truth untouched) and record the coverage lost by
the AV2-calibrated threshold: (i) \emph{2\,Hz history}: keep the five 2\,Hz keyframes of the 2\,s history and interpolate linearly; (ii) \emph{lane dropout}: drop each map polyline with the probability implied
by the measured median number of lanes near the target agent (nuScenes 52 vs.\ AV2 85); (iii) both; (iv) \emph{scene mix}: re-weight AV2 scenes to the nuScenes distribution of seven label-free scene
factors with cross-fitted domain-classifier weights, which isolates pure covariate shift. Two further candidates were \emph{measured} and excluded: excess history noise
(a quadratic-fit residual estimate is smaller on nuScenes: $0.13$\,m vs.\ $0.29$\,m) and map-point density (equal per scene).

\subsection{Label-free shift monitor}
A domain classifier on label-free scene features (native sampling interval, nearby-agent count, map density, nearby
lanes, history speed, heading change, curvature) is trained to separate source and target scenes; its AUC is the monitor.
Within a dataset, the same classifier is trained per city against the rest. We test whether the AUC predicts the coverage loss
(across cities, by Spearman correlation).
