\begin{abstract}
A conformal prediction region promises to contain a road user's true future path a fixed fraction of the time. We ask
whether that promise survives when a trajectory forecaster is calibrated on one driving dataset and deployed, without
new labels, on another. Using forecasters calibrated on Argoverse 2 or nuScenes and evaluated on each other and on
Waymo Open Motion, we measure coverage losses of 6 to 25 percentage points at a 90 percent nominal level. The losses
are confirmed on two architectures matching published accuracy, registered in advance for the third dataset. Within
one architecture family a more accurate model loses more coverage, but this does not hold across architectures.
Reweighting the calibration data by
an estimated covariate-shift likelihood ratio makes the loss worse, and we show that no label-free method can repair
an unrestricted shift. The loss concentrates in turns, most of all in left-hand traffic, and measured input
differences between the datasets explain a quarter of it for one architecture and all of it for the other. Labelled target data can repair it, but labels
arrive one scene at a time, and a guarantee that treats agents as independent can fail when scenes contain many
agents, by an amount that depends on scene size and on how wrong the source threshold already was. A scene-level
Learn-then-Test rule with a
betting test restores the guarantee at every labelling budget we tested, and returns a region within twice the oracle
size from about 150 to 200 labelled scenes. Conformal guarantees for trajectory prediction should therefore not be
assumed to transfer across datasets without an audit and a scene-level recalibration.
\end{abstract}
