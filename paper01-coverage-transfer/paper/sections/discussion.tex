\section{Discussion}
A planner that trusts a source-calibrated region on a new dataset trusts a region that is too small. At $\alpha=0.1$
the confirmatory models lose \gapRawConf, \gapRawConfRev, \wmGapAvW\ and \wmGapNsW\ points of coverage on the four
transfers we tested, and the loss is silent: nothing observable without labels signals it. Four results bear
directly on practice.

\emph{Label-free fixes are not safe defaults.} Covariate reweighting made the gap larger on every forward model, and
the normalised score helped in one direction and hurt in the other. A practitioner who applies either and stops can be
worse off than one who does nothing.

\emph{Auditing is cheap; repairing is not.} About $10^3$ labelled target agents suffice to detect a gap of this size
with high power. Repairing it with a guarantee that holds under scene clustering needs whole labelled scenes: roughly
\recMMin\ to \recMMax\ of them before the region is both finite and within twice the oracle area. Below that budget the
correct behaviour is to abstain, which the scene-level rule does.

\emph{A learned predictor of scene loss does not replace labels.} A classifier trained on source labels to predict whether an agent is
missed explained at most \predBestRsq\% of the variance of the true per-scene miss fraction on any target, and also little on held-out
source scenes, so it cannot stand in for target labels, for example as a control variate that shortens the label budget.

\emph{The unit of labelling matters, and scene size alone does not predict the failure.} On nuScenes (mean \nsAgentsMean\ agents
per scene), agent-level guarantees were nearly useless for AutoBot (\agentCorNsForty\% valid at $m=40$) but mostly
valid for Wayformer on the identical scenes (\agentCorWfForty\%, Sec.~\ref{sec:wf}), tracking how badly each
model's source threshold was miscalibrated there (\rSrcAutobot\% vs.\ \rSrcWayformer\% missed), not the scene
structure, which was the same for both. Agent-level guarantees did hold on Waymo and AV2, where scenes are small,
for every model we tried. A practitioner cannot know in advance which case they are in without the audit of
Sec.~\ref{sec:repair-scene}, which is the practical argument for using the scene-level guarantee by default rather
than hoping agent-level recalibration happens to be close enough.

\paragraph{Mechanism} Two observations narrow the cause. For AutoBot the measured input differences explain about a
quarter of the gap, and the loss concentrates in turns under left-hand traffic, which AV2 does not contain; for that
model both point to a change in the conditional behaviour of road users rather than in the scenes themselves,
consistent with Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}. The turn pattern replicates on Wayformer, but the input-level
explanation does not: its injected input differences reproduce the whole, smaller gap (Sec.~\ref{sec:wf}), so how much
of the loss is protocol and how much is behaviour depends on the model. The exploratory AutoBot zoo showed
the most accurate model losing the most coverage; Wayformer breaks this pattern, with accuracy comparable to the
confirmatory AutoBot model but under half its nuScenes gap. Accuracy is therefore not a general predictor of gap
size across architectures, only a pattern observed within one architecture family trained several ways.

\paragraph{Limitations} (i) Two architectures, one trained in both directions; Wayformer confirms the direction, reweighting,
turn and repair findings but was only trained forward, so its reverse direction is untested, and its injection result
rests on reconstructed kinematics.
(ii) For AutoBot the measured input differences leave most of the gap unexplained, and unmeasured
causes cannot be ruled out. (iii) Coverage is marginal over agents or scenes, not conditional on a scenario; the
manoeuvre breakdown is a diagnostic, not a guarantee. (iv) Proposition~\ref{prop:ident} covers scale shifts only.
(v) The repair budget is measured on three datasets and may differ on others; the registered evaluation design
(disjoint pools, exact evaluation) should be reused rather than the numbers. (vi) Seeds: a second training seed of the confirmatory AutoBot model gave an AV2$\to$nuScenes
loss of \seedGapOne\ points (95\% interval \seedCiLo--\seedCiHi) against \seedGapZero\ for the first, meeting our registered
criterion (same sign, within a factor of two) but showing that seed-to-seed variation exceeds the sampling interval of
a single run. Other transfers and Wayformer were not re-seeded.

\section{Conclusion}
A conformal guarantee for trajectory prediction does not transfer across driving datasets. On three datasets, two
architectures and six source-target pairs, a forecaster calibrated at 90\% covered between \covTgtMin\ and
\covTgtMax\% of target futures, every time under-covering, never over. Reweighting does not repair the loss and no
label-free method can in general. Within one architecture, more accurate models lost more coverage; across
architectures this did not hold, so accuracy is not a general predictor of how badly the guarantee fails. Labelled
target data can repair it: a scene-level Learn-then-Test rule with a correctly capped betting $p$-value restores the
guarantee at every budget we tested, on both architectures, and gives a usable region from about \recMMin\ labelled
scenes. We recommend that any transferred conformal region be audited on target labels and, where it fails,
recalibrated at the scene level before it is used for planning.
