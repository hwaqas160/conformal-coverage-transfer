\IEEEPARstart{A}{utonomous} vehicles plan around forecasts of what nearby road users will do. A forecast is only as
useful as the statement it makes about its own reliability. A planner that knows a predicted region contains the true
future with probability at least $1-\alpha$ can keep clear of that region and of nothing larger. Conformal prediction
(CP) supplies such a statement for any black-box predictor, with a finite-sample guarantee that needs only
exchangeability of calibration and test data \cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}, and it is now used for
trajectory prediction and safe planning \cite{lindemann2023safe,sun2024copula,huang2025cuqds,lindemann2024formal}.

Deployment breaks exchangeability. A forecaster is calibrated on the data at hand, with one set of cities, one sensor
suite and one annotation pipeline, and is then driven somewhere else. Trajectory predictors lose accuracy when moved
between datasets \cite{feng2024unitraj,demmler2025dataset}, and model calibration degrades under dataset shift
\cite{ovadia2019can}. What is not known is what happens to the \emph{guarantee itself} when a conformally calibrated
forecaster is moved, without new labels, to a different real driving dataset: whether the stated $1-\alpha$ survives,
how badly it fails, and whether the standard remedies repair it. Weighted CP needs the likelihood ratio
\cite{tibshirani2019covariate}, distance-based bounds need the size of the shift \cite{barber2023beyond,aolaritei2025levy},
and online methods need a stream of labels \cite{gibbs2021adaptive,angelopoulos2023pid}. None settles the static,
label-free, dataset-to-dataset case that Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} illustrates.

\paragraph*{Contributions}
(i) A direct measurement of zero-label conformal coverage transfer between real driving datasets, in both directions
between Argoverse~2 and nuScenes and, registered before it was run, on Waymo Open Motion, which to our knowledge no
prior work has quantified at this scale. Coverage falls by \gapRawWayformer\ to \wmGapWf\ points at a nominal 90\%, confirmed on
two architectures within 15\% of published accuracy; across training budgets of one architecture the more accurate models lose more
coverage, but this does not hold between training seeds or across architectures. (ii) A diagnosis. Covariate reweighting makes the loss
worse, a non-identifiability result (Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}) shows why label-free repair is limited in
principle, the loss concentrates in turns in left-hand traffic, and controlled input perturbations account for part of it. (iii) A repair protocol, not a new
conformal algorithm: it applies Learn-then-Test with a betting test at the scene level, which gives a finite-sample,
training-conditional guarantee when labels arrive scene by scene (Propositions~\ref{prop:cor} and~\ref{prop:scene}).
It is valid at every labelling budget we tested, on both
architectures, and gives a usable region from about \recMMin\ labelled scenes, while the natural agent-level
guarantee can fail when scenes contain many agents and the source threshold is already badly wrong. (iv) Code,
registered hypotheses and an append-only experiment log, including a defect found and corrected before submission.
