\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Conformal prediction for trajectories.}
Split CP turns any predictor into one with marginal coverage guarantees \cite{vovk2005algorithmic,angelopoulos2023gentle}
and underlies conformal safe planning around predicted agents \cite{lindemann2023safe}. Multi-step and multi-modal
trajectories have been handled with copula-based intervals \cite{sun2024copula}, scenario-aware intervals in a Frenet
frame on nuPlan with a learned reliability discriminator \cite{shu2025scenario}, and multi-view calibration that
fuses vehicle- and infrastructure-side
observations before conformal calibration \cite{chen2024v2i}. A 2025 survey of the field catalogues these directions
and the open gaps around uncertainty and generalisation \cite{abdelmadjid2025survey}; concurrent architecture work
targets multimodal mode decoding \cite{zhou2026modeseq} and a shared encoder across simulation, prediction and
planning \cite{song2025unimotion}, and a controlled dataset-design study on a new benchmark finds that feature
engineering matters less than which data the model sees \cite{demmler2025dataset} --- a finding our Sec.~\ref{sec:where}
echoes from the calibration side. Calibrating the predictive \emph{distribution} itself, rather than only the
output region, has also been proposed \cite{pouria2026gaussian}. Locally weighted scores that divide the residual by
an estimated spread \cite{lei2018distribution} are the classical route to adaptive regions; our normalised score uses
the forecaster's own predicted scale in that role, an LLM-based safe-navigation system uses a related idea in token
space \cite{doula2025safepath}.

\paragraph{Safe planning and robot safety with conformal guarantees.}
Beyond trajectory regions, conformal prediction has been used to certify learned perception for navigation
\cite{mei2024perceive}, to adapt a crowd-navigation policy's safety margin to distribution shift
\cite{yao2025crowdnav}, to learn safety regions from sparse human feedback \cite{feldman2025robotsafety}, to certify
learned dynamical-system motion primitives \cite{binny2026stable}, and more broadly to connect conformal prediction
to formal verification and control of learning-enabled autonomous systems \cite{lindemann2024formal}. Closest to our
label-free-versus-labelled distinction, \cite{mirzaeedodangeh2025interactive} transfers a safety guarantee across
policy updates analytically rather than by relabelling, and \cite{binny2025moved} treats the distribution shift the
ego agent itself induces in interacting agents. All of these assume the calibration and deployment \emph{domain}
match; we study what happens when the domain itself changes.

\paragraph{Conformal prediction under shift.}
Weighted CP handles covariate shift given the likelihood ratio \cite{tibshirani2019covariate}, and the coverage loss
under general shift can be bounded by a distance between calibration and test distributions \cite{barber2023beyond}.
Recent work replaces that bound with an explicit ambiguity-set radius under L\'{e}vy--Prokhorov perturbations
\cite{aolaritei2025levy}, regularises the representation with a Wasserstein penalty to shrink the achievable coverage
gap \cite{xu2025wasserstein}, or uses optimal transport with unlabelled target data to both estimate and mitigate the
loss in coverage \cite{correia2025nonexchangeable}; a parallel line gives sample-complexity lower bounds for when a
selective predictor can certify a risk floor under covariate shift at all \cite{liu2026certify}, a negative result in
the same spirit as our Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}. Online methods adapt the threshold from realised errors:
adaptive conformal inference \cite{gibbs2021adaptive}, conformal PID control \cite{angelopoulos2023pid}, its use for
trajectories in CUQDS \cite{huang2025cuqds}, and adaptive nonconformity scores with a replay buffer
\cite{tumu2026adaptnc}. More recent online variants retroactively revise past predictions for faster adaptation
\cite{jun2026retrospective}, cast the threshold update as a universal-portfolio game \cite{liu2026portfolio}, or give
training-conditional (not just time-averaged) regret bounds under drift \cite{liang2026optimalregret}; all of these
still need a stream of ground-truth labels after deployment, which is exactly what our zero-label setting excludes.
Other work conditions on an observable nuisance parameter and generates synthetic data with a diffusion model
\cite{rahaman2026shift} or treats the shift the ego vehicle itself induces in other agents \cite{binny2025moved}
(both preprints). We study a different point in this space: no feedback, no generative model, and three real driving
datasets.

\paragraph{Grouped, hierarchical and conditional coverage.}
When observations come in groups, exchangeability across observations fails, and hierarchical conformal methods
restore validity by pooling or subsampling at the group level \cite{dunn2023hierarchical,lee2023hierarchical}; recent
extensions handle hierarchically structured outputs with a reconciliation step \cite{principato2024hierarchicaldata},
weight groups directly when the shift is known to act at the group level \cite{bhattacharyya2024groupweighted}, carry
constrained-complexity representations into hierarchical classification \cite{mortier2025hierarchicalclass}, or let a
test group borrow structure from a donor group when only partial test-group data is available
\cite{mallick2026generalizedhcp}. A broader conditional-coverage literature asks for validity on subpopulations or
individual test points rather than only on average: a unifying decomposition of conditional miscoverage
\cite{min2026unifiedtheory}, learned reliability estimators that predict where coverage will fail
\cite{zhou2026cpassessment,braun2025diagnostics}, group-conditional coverage over overlapping, fractional group
memberships \cite{bairaktari2025kandinsky}, an audit showing nominal marginal coverage can still silently fail a
specific clinical subgroup \cite{zhong2026fairness}, and conditional risk control for segmentation via a weighted-quantile
connection to risk control \cite{luo2025conditionalrisk}. Risk-controlling procedures \cite{bates2021rcps} and
Learn-then-Test \cite{angelopoulos2025ltt} give finite-sample control of a monotone risk by testing a fixed ordered
set of thresholds, and betting-based $p$-values \cite{waudbysmith2024betting} make such tests far tighter than
Hoeffding-type bounds when losses are small. We combine the two ideas for a setting those works do not address:
dataset shift in trajectory prediction, where the natural labelling unit is the \emph{scene} and the risk to be
controlled is the scene-averaged miscoverage of a fixed, source-calibrated forecaster; our Addendum~D (revision R1,
reported in Sec.~\ref{sec:confirm}) is itself a cautionary case study in exactly the betting-validity failure mode
that \cite{alvarez2024pvalue} studies in general, for bounded-variable $p$-values.

\paragraph{Risk control and betting, more generally.}
Beyond Learn-then-Test, risk control has been extended to adaptive, data-dependent tradeoffs among several risks at
once \cite{nguyen2024tradeoffs}, to a semi-supervised setting that uses unlabelled data to tune the risk-control
hyperparameter without extra labels \cite{einbinder2024semisupervised}, and to post-processing an existing decision
policy only where a risk budget is violated, rather than replacing it outright \cite{joshi2026riskcontrolled}.
E-values have been proposed as a more flexible alternative to the $p$-values we use, enabling anytime-valid and
fixed-size variants \cite{gauthier2025evalues}; weighted-conformal test martingales extend betting-style monitoring
to post-deployment shift detection that distinguishes benign covariate shift from harmful shift
\cite{prinster2025watch}; and \cite{vovk2025randomness} revisits the randomness-theoretic foundations that justify
treating a conformal predictor as valid for i.i.d., not only exchangeable, data. We use the same betting machinery
for a different purpose: certifying a \emph{scene-averaged} risk from whole labelled scenes.

\paragraph{Cross-dataset trajectory prediction and perception under domain shift.}
UniTraj harmonises several driving datasets and reports that accuracy drops when models are transferred between them
\cite{feng2024unitraj}; that study is about point accuracy, not about the validity of an uncertainty guarantee.
Beyond trajectories, \cite{ovadia2019can} showed that calibration of deep models degrades under dataset shift; our
question is the conformal analogue for structured, multi-modal forecasts. The same cross-dataset gap is documented
on the perception side of the autonomous-driving stack: visual localisation uncertainty is miscalibrated under novel
weather and lighting \cite{wu2025visuallocalization}, collaborative-perception benchmarks struggle to generalise
across sensor rigs and sites \cite{wang2025collaborative}, cross-sensor pretraining is used specifically to close a
domain gap that would otherwise need target-domain labels \cite{kong2025largead,qiu2026crosssensor}, and a broader
uncertainty-aware perception survey argues for explicit, communicable uncertainty rather than a single calibrated
score \cite{manchingal2025uncertaintyaware}. None of this literature asks whether a \emph{conformal} guarantee
specifically, rather than calibration quality in general, survives the same transfer; that is the question this
paper measures, diagnoses and partially repairs.
