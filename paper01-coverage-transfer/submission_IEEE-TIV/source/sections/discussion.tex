\section{Discussion}
A planner that trusts a source-calibrated region on a new dataset trusts a region that is too small. At $\alpha=0.1$
the confirmatory models lose \gapRawConf, \gapRawConfRev, \wmGapAvW\ and \wmGapNsW\ points of coverage on the four
transfers we tested, and the loss is silent: nothing observable without labels signals it. Three results bear
directly on practice.

\emph{Label-free fixes are not safe defaults.} Covariate reweighting made the gap larger on every forward model, and
the normalised score helped in one direction and hurt in the other. A practitioner who applies either and stops can be
worse off than one who does nothing.

\emph{Auditing is cheap; repairing is not.} About $10^3$ labelled target agents suffice to detect a gap of this size
with high power. Repairing it with a guarantee that holds under scene clustering needs whole labelled scenes: roughly
\recMMin\ to \recMMax\ of them before the region is both finite and within twice the oracle area. Below that budget the
correct behaviour is to abstain, which the scene-level rule does.

\emph{The unit of labelling matters.} Agent-level guarantees failed exactly where scenes are large (nuScenes, mean 65 and median 53
agents per scene) and held where they are small (Waymo, AV2). Any certification protocol for driving data should
state its labelling unit and resample at that unit.

\paragraph{Mechanism} Two observations narrow the cause. The measured input differences explain about a quarter of
the gap, and the loss concentrates in turns under left-hand traffic, which AV2 does not contain. Both point to a change
in the conditional behaviour of road users rather than in the scenes themselves, consistent with
Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}. That the most accurate model shows the largest gap is consistent with a sharper,
more confident source fit that extrapolates worse; testing this needs more architectures than we trained.

\paragraph{Limitations} (i) One architecture family; the conclusion that accuracy and coverage validity can diverge
should be tested on others. (ii) The measured input differences leave most of the gap unexplained, and unmeasured
causes cannot be ruled out. (iii) Coverage is marginal over agents or scenes, not conditional on a scenario; the
manoeuvre breakdown is a diagnostic, not a guarantee. (iv) Proposition~\ref{prop:ident} covers scale shifts only.
(v) The repair budget is measured on three datasets and may differ on others; the registered evaluation design
(disjoint pools, exact evaluation) should be reused rather than the numbers.

\section{Conclusion}
A conformal guarantee for trajectory prediction does not transfer across driving datasets. On three datasets and four
transfer pairs, a forecaster calibrated at 90\% covered between \covTgtMin\ and \covTgtMax\% of target futures, and a
more accurate forecaster lost more coverage, not less. Reweighting does not repair the loss and no label-free method can
in general. Labelled target data can: a scene-level Learn-then-Test rule with a correctly capped betting $p$-value
restores the guarantee at every budget we tested and gives a usable region from about \recMMin\ labelled scenes. We
recommend that any transferred conformal region be audited on target labels and, where it fails, recalibrated at the
scene level before it is used for planning.
