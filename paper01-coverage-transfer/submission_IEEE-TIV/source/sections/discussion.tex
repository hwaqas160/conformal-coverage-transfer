\section{Discussion}
A planner that trusts a source-calibrated region on a new dataset trusts a region that is too small. At $\alpha=0.1$
the confirmatory models lose \gapRawConf, \gapRawConfRev, \wmGapAvW\ and \wmGapNsW\ points of coverage on the four
transfers we tested. Without labels the loss cannot be measured, although the drift of the forecaster's predicted scale
warns of it (Sec.~V-D). Four results bear directly on practice.

\emph{Label-free fixes are not safe defaults.} Reweighting made every forward gap larger, and the normalised score
helped in one direction and hurt in the other.

\emph{Auditing is cheap; repairing is not.} About $10^3$ labelled agents detect a gap of this size; repairing it with a
guarantee that survives scene clustering needs roughly \recMMin\ to \recMMax\ whole scenes before the region is finite and
within twice the oracle area, and below that budget the right behaviour is to abstain (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol}).

\emph{A learned predictor of scene loss does not replace labels.} A source-trained classifier of whether an agent is
missed explained at most \predBestRsq\% of the variance of the per-scene miss fraction on any target.

\emph{The unit of labelling matters, and scene size alone does not predict the failure.} On nuScenes (mean
\nsAgentsMean\ agents per scene), agent-level guarantees were nearly useless for AutoBot (\agentCorNsForty\% valid at
$m=40$) but mostly valid for Wayformer on the identical scenes (\agentCorWfForty\%, Sec.~\ref{sec:wf}), tracking how
badly each model's source threshold was miscalibrated there (\rSrcAutobot\% vs.\ \rSrcWayformer\% missed), not the
scene structure, which was the same for both. Agent-level guarantees held on AV2 and on Waymo where scenes are small
and the source threshold less wrong, but not for nuScenes$\to$Waymo (\agentCorNsWmThreeHundred\% valid at $m=300$),
whose source threshold misses \rSrcNsWm\% of agents. Without labels a practitioner cannot tell which case applies, so the scene-level guarantee should be the default.

\paragraph{What a planner would see} Descriptive, agent-weighted numbers (no guarantee covers them): at the source
threshold, \missAgentsAvNs\% of nuScenes agents and \missAgentsAvWm\% of Waymo agents fall outside the AV2-calibrated
region, against a nominal 10\%, and the shortfall is concentrated in turns: \missRightSg\% of right-turning agents in
Singapore and \missRightBos\% in Boston, against \missStraightBos\% of straight-driving agents in Boston. The tube is
\qSrcAvNs\,m wide where \qOracleAvNs\,m is needed; scene-level repair restores \recQAvNs\,m.

\paragraph{Mechanism} For AutoBot the measured input differences reproduce about a quarter of the gap, and the loss concentrates in
turns under left-hand traffic, which AV2 does not contain; we read this as a change in the conditional behaviour of road
users rather than in the scenes (an interpretation, consistent with Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}). The turn pattern replicates
on Wayformer but the input-level explanation does not: its injected differences reproduce the whole, smaller gap
(Sec.~\ref{sec:wf}), so how much of the loss is protocol and how much behaviour depends on the model. The accuracy--gap
association across AutoBot training budgets breaks between seeds and across architectures, so accuracy is not a general
predictor of the gap.

\paragraph{Limitations} (i) Two architectures; Wayformer was only trained forward and its injection result rests on
reconstructed kinematics. (ii) For AutoBot the measured input differences leave most of the gap unexplained. (iii) Coverage is
marginal over agents or scenes, not conditional on a scenario; the manoeuvre breakdown is a diagnostic. (iv)
Proposition~\ref{prop:ident} covers scale shifts only. (v) The repair budget is measured on three datasets and may differ on
others; reuse the registered design, not the numbers. (vi) Seeds: a second AutoBot seed gave an AV2$\to$nuScenes loss of
\seedGapOne\ points (95\% interval \seedCiLo--\seedCiHi) against \seedGapZero\ (\seedZeroCiLo--\seedZeroCiHi), meeting our
registered criterion (same sign, within a factor of two); seed-to-seed variation is as large as one run's sampling
uncertainty. A second Wayformer seed (accuracy \wfSeedAde) gave \wfSeedGapOne\ points on nuScenes against \wfSeedGapZero\ and
\wfSeedWmOne\ against \wfSeedWmZero\ on Waymo, so its smaller nuScenes gap is not a seed artefact. The reverse direction,
the injections, recalibration and the multi-dataset model were not re-seeded.

\section{Conclusion}
A conformal guarantee for trajectory prediction does not transfer across driving datasets. On three datasets, two
architectures and six model--target transfers, a forecaster calibrated at 90\% covered between \covTgtMin\ and
\covTgtMax\% of target futures, every time under-covering, never over. Reweighting does not repair the loss and no
label-free method can in general, though the drift of the forecaster's predicted scale warns of it, and training on
two datasets cuts the loss on a third by about a third without removing it. More accurate AutoBot models lost more coverage across training budgets, but not
between seeds or across architectures, so accuracy is not a general predictor of how badly the guarantee fails.
Labelled target data can repair it: a scene-level Learn-then-Test rule with a correctly capped betting $p$-value
restores the guarantee at every budget we tested, on both architectures, and gives a usable region from about
\recMMin\ labelled scenes. We recommend that any transferred conformal region be audited on target labels and, where it
fails, recalibrated at the scene level (Fig.~\ref{fig:protocol}) before it is used for planning.
