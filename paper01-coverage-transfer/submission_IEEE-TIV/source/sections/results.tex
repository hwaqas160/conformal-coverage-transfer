\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
Confirmatory results come first, organised by the four questions of Sec.~\ref{sec:problem}. The reduced-budget
models on which the analyses were designed are summarised in Sec.~\ref{sec:expl}.

\subsection{Q1: How large is the gap?}\label{sec:confirm}
On its own test split each confirmatory model is calibrated as promised: the same-domain gap is \gapSameConf\ points
for the AV2 model and $\gapSameConfRev$ points for the nuScenes model (Fig.~\ref{fig:gap}). On every other dataset it
under-covers. Calibrated on AV2, coverage falls by \gapRawConf\ points on nuScenes and by \wmGapAvW\ points on Waymo.
Calibrated on nuScenes, it falls by \gapRawConfRev\ points on AV2 and by \wmGapNsW\ points on Waymo. All four
intervals exclude zero by a wide margin, so a Holm correction over the four registered tests changes no conclusion.
The Waymo tests were registered before any Waymo prediction was computed. In metres, the AV2-calibrated tube has radius
\qSrcAvNs\,m, while 90\% coverage needs \qOracleAvNs\,m on nuScenes and \qOracleAvWm\,m on Waymo: the source region is
less than half as wide as the target requires.

The nuScenes test agents come from only \nsNScenes\ scenes, so they are strongly dependent: resampling whole scenes
inflates the variance by a design effect of \designEffConf, so all intervals on multi-agent targets resample scenes. Across training budgets the more accurate model has the larger gap: the confirmatory AV2
model, within \confAccRelPct\% of UniTraj's published accuracy, loses \gapRawConf\ points, against \explGapMin\ to
\explGapMax\ points for the three reduced-budget AV2 models (Table~\ref{tab:models}). The pattern does not hold between
training seeds: a second seed of the same recipe is slightly less accurate (\seedOneAde) yet loses \seedGapOne\ points.
It is an association across budgets of one architecture family, not a causal claim.

\subsection{Q2: Does covariate reweighting fix it?}\label{sec:h2h3}
No. Weighting the calibration scores by an estimated, label-free likelihood ratio increased the gap on every
forward model by about two points (column ``rew.'' in Table~\ref{tab:models}) and left the reverse direction unchanged.
Reweighting AV2 scenes to the nuScenes mix of speeds and manoeuvres even moves coverage the other way
(Table~\ref{tab:inject}): nuScenes' scene mix is easier, so composition cannot explain the loss. This is the pattern
expected when the conditional distribution of the future given the scene changes, which no reweighting on the scene
can repair (Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}).

\subsection{Where does the loss come from?}\label{sec:where}
The loss is concentrated in turns. On the confirmatory model, right-turn coverage falls by \dropRightConf\ points from
AV2 to nuScenes, against \dropStraightConf\ points for straight driving (Fig.~\ref{fig:traj}). It is largest in
Singapore, where traffic drives on the left: the right-turn drop there exceeds the drop in Boston by
\sgExtraRightConf\ points. What AV2-trained forecasters miss is the wide, across-traffic turn of left-hand traffic.
For AutoBot, injecting one measured input difference at a time into AV2 test inputs reproduces only part of the gap
(Table~\ref{tab:inject}): a 2\,Hz history explains \injHzConf\%, lane dropout \injMapConf\%, and both together about
a quarter. Excess annotation noise and map-point density were measured and found absent.

\subsection{Q3: What can label-free methods do?}\label{sec:labelfree}
Little, and not reliably. The model-normalised score (Sec.~\ref{sec:norm}) reduces the AV2$\to$nuScenes gap by
\normReductionConf\% for AutoBot (to \gapNormConf\ points) and by \normReductionWf\% for Wayformer, but widens the
reverse gap (to \gapNormConfRev\ points). On the
reduced-budget models its effect ranges from a \explNormBestRed\% reduction to a \explNormWorstInc\% increase. A domain-classifier shift monitor
correlates with per-city coverage loss at Spearman $\rho\le\monitorRhoMax$ on every model, below the registered bar of 0.5.
Adaptive conformal inference \cite{gibbs2021adaptive} converges only with a stream of labels; frozen after 1000 labels,
its threshold covers $\ge90\%$ of held-out scenes in only \aciFrozenMin--\aciFrozenMax\% of streams. These results
agree with Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}: without target labels there is no general fix.

One label-free method restricts the shift class, as that proposition says a useful one must. \emph{Protocol-matched
recalibration} passes the source calibration inputs through the measured AV2$\to$nuScenes input differences of
Sec.~\ref{sec:inject} (2\,Hz history and lane dropout, estimated from target inputs only) and recalibrates on the
result. It is exploratory and has no guarantee. For AutoBot it moves the threshold from \mbQSrcAb\ to \mbQPmAb\,m
and coverage on nuScenes from \mbCovSrcAb\% to \mbCovPmAb\%, closing \mbClosedAb\% of the gap. For Wayformer it moves it
from \mbQSrcWf\ to \mbQPmWf\,m, coverage from \mbCovSrcWf\% to \mbCovPmWf\%, and overshoots the gap to
$\mbGapPmWf$ points, in the conservative direction, which may partly reflect how Wayformer's velocity inputs are
reconstructed for injected trajectories (Sec.~\ref{sec:wf}). It does not shorten the label budget of Sec.~\ref{sec:repair-scene}:
started from the matched threshold, the scene-level rule still needs $m=\mbRecMAb$ scenes (AutoBot) and $m=\mbRecMWf$
(Wayformer) for a usable region. A measured correction recovers part of the gap but does not replace labels.

The loss is not an artefact of the registered score. Re-scored with four alternatives (exploratory: top-probability
mode, final displacement, probability-weighted, and per-time-step scaling by the source median error), all \scCells\
out-of-domain cases under-cover, by \scGapMin\ to \scGapMax\ points. No score is uniformly better: the time-scaled score cuts
AutoBot's AV2$\to$Waymo loss from \scRegWmAb\ to \scNormWmAb\ points but raises its AV2$\to$nuScenes loss from \scRegNsAb\
to \scNormNsAb.

Two further label-free constructions, registered before running, also fail. \emph{Worst-domain calibration} (the largest
threshold over the other labelled datasets) meets its bar (gap $\le2$ points at $\le2\times$ the oracle area) in only
\abAok\ of six held-out cells: it over-covers the easier targets by up to \abEasyOverMax\ points and under-covers Waymo,
the hardest dataset, by \abWmGapMin--\abWmGapMax\ points. \emph{Manoeuvre-conditional calibration}, with groups from history
only, closes a median of \abBRedMed\% of the gap (bar met in \abBok\ of six). A restricted shift class helps only if the
target belongs to it (Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}).

\subsection{Q4: How many labels to audit and to repair?}\label{sec:repair-scene}
We report three label budgets: to \emph{detect} the failure, to \emph{certify} a repaired threshold, and for that
threshold to be \emph{useful}. The second does not imply the third.

\emph{Audit (detect).} With $k=1000$ labelled target agents the exact binomial audit (Sec.~\ref{sec:audit}) detects the gap
with probability \auditPowerConfMin\ on every confirmatory run (Table~\ref{tab:models}). On a shift-free split it rejects in
\auditFaAllMin--\auditFaAllMax\% of label draws. This is not excess Type-I error: the audit tests the realised source
threshold, which itself under-covers its own test split by up to \auditSameGapMax\ points when its calibration draw is
unlucky, and the rejection rate tracks that shortfall. A deployment that only wants to flag shifts should test
$1-\alpha-\epsilon$ for a small margin $\epsilon$.

\emph{Repair with exchangeable labels (certify).} When $k=1000$ agents are labelled at random, certify-or-recalibrate
(Sec.~\ref{sec:cor}) covers $\ge90\%$ of held-out agents in \corCORMin--\corCORMax\% of label draws, against
\corDirectMin--\corDirectMax\% for plain recalibration, at a region \corCORareaMin--\corCORareaMax$\times$ the oracle's.

\emph{Repair with whole-scene labels (certify at the scene level).} Real labels arrive scene by scene, which breaks agent-level exchangeability. We
draw $m$ labelled scenes from a pool disjoint from the evaluation scenes (nuScenes train for AV2$\to$nuScenes, the
Waymo calibration half for the Waymo targets) and evaluate the scene-averaged miscoverage $R(\hat q)$ exactly on the
evaluation pool, 1000 draws per $m$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:scene}). The scene-level rule with the betting $p$-value is valid at
every $m$ on all four transfers ($\Pr[R(\hat q)\le\alpha]\ge\betValidMin$). The agent-level rule is not: on
AV2$\to$nuScenes, with a mean of \nsAgentsMean\ agents per scene (median \nsAgentsMedian), it is valid in \agentCorNsForty\% of draws at $m=40$ and
\agentCorNsThreeHundred\% at $m=300$, and one-agent-per-scene split CP \cite{dunn2023hierarchical} in
\hierNsThreeHundred\%. With few agents per scene it stays valid in at least \agentCorSmallMin\% of draws for AV2$\to$Waymo,
nuScenes$\to$AV2 and Wayformer's AV2$\to$Waymo, but not for nuScenes$\to$Waymo, where it falls from
\agentCorNsWmForty\% at $m=40$ to \agentCorNsWmThreeHundred\% at $m=300$ although Waymo scenes are small: there the
source threshold misses \rSrcNsWm\% of evaluation agents. Agent-level guarantees fail where scenes are large or the
source threshold is badly wrong (Sec.~\ref{sec:wf}).

\emph{Useful.} Validity is not usefulness. With $m\le40$ scenes the scene-level rule abstains in every draw, correctly reporting that
it cannot certify any finite threshold rather than returning an unsupported one. The abstention rate falls with $m$
but is not negligible close to the recommended budget: on AV2$\to$nuScenes it is \infAtEightyAvNs\% at $m=80$ and still
\infAtHundredAvNs\% at $m=100$. It returns a usable region (at most twice the oracle area, abstaining in
$\le5\%$ of draws) from $m=\recMAvNs$ scenes for AV2$\to$nuScenes, AV2$\to$Waymo and nuScenes$\to$Waymo, and from
$m=\recMNsAv$ for nuScenes$\to$AV2, whose single-agent scenes carry less information each. At that budget the repaired
AV2$\to$nuScenes radius is \recQAvNs\,m against an oracle of \qOracleAvNs\,m. The betting $p$-value gives smaller regions
than Hoeffding--Bentkus at large $m$ (\betAreaNsTwoHundred$\times$ vs.\ \hbAreaNsTwoHundred$\times$ the oracle area at
$m=200$ on AV2$\to$nuScenes). In dataset terms, \recMAvNs\ scenes is more than the \nsNScenes\ scenes of the entire nuScenes
validation split, so on that target the repair draws on training-split labels, which assumes nuScenes training and
validation scenes are exchangeable. A deployment that can label fewer scenes should expect the rule to abstain, not to
return a smaller guaranteed region.

\subsection{A second architecture: Wayformer}\label{sec:wf}
To test whether the findings are AutoBot-specific we trained Wayformer \cite{nayakanti2023wayformer} on the same full
AV2 set under the same gate (val minADE\textsubscript{6} \minADESixWayformer\ for the final checkpoint; UniTraj reports 0.85 for both
architectures \cite[Table~15]{feng2024unitraj}), forward only.

The direction replicates everywhere: same-domain gap \gapSameWayformer\ points; AV2$\to$nuScenes gap
$+\gapRawWayformer$ points (CI \gapRawWayformerCI); AV2$\to$Waymo gap $+\wmGapWf$ points (CI \wmGapWfCI). Reweighting
again makes the nuScenes gap worse (\rewWayformer\ points); the audit has power \auditPowerWayformer. The loss again
concentrates in turns (right-turn drop \dropRightWf\ points vs.\
\dropStraightWf\ for straight) and in Singapore (\sgExtraRightWf\ points more than Boston), with a similar scene-cluster
design effect (\designEffWf\ vs.\ AutoBot's \designEffConf). The scene-level repair is valid at every $m$ on both
Wayformer transfers (Fig.~\ref{fig:scene}b) and reaches the same operating point as AutoBot: $m=\recMWfNs$ scenes for
AV2$\to$nuScenes (area \recAreaWfNs$\times$) and $m=\recMWfWm$ for AV2$\to$Waymo (area \recAreaWfWm$\times$).

The input-level explanation does not replicate in size. Injecting the 2\,Hz history costs Wayformer \wfInjHz\ points
(\wfInjHzFrac\% of its nuScenes gap), and with lane dropout \wfInjBoth\ points (\wfInjBothFrac\%), against about a quarter
for AutoBot. Wayformer reads velocity, acceleration and heading channels that AutoBot ignores; for an injected
trajectory we reconstruct them by finite difference, which gives a piecewise-constant velocity that real 2\,Hz sensing
would not have and may overstate the effect. Lane dropout, which needs no reconstruction, still accounts for \wfInjMapFrac\%.

Magnitude does not replicate. Wayformer's AV2$\to$nuScenes gap is under half of AutoBot's confirmatory
\gapRawConf\ points, while its AV2$\to$Waymo gap ($+\wmGapWf$) is close to AutoBot's ($+\wmGapAvW$). This breaks the
``more accurate model, larger gap'' pattern (Sec.~\ref{sec:confirm}): Wayformer is about as accurate as AutoBot's
confirmatory model, yet its nuScenes gap is far smaller. A second, unregistered difference: on the \emph{same} nuScenes
target, agent-level certify-or-recalibrate was almost useless for AutoBot (\agentCorNsForty\% valid at $m=40$,
\agentCorNsThreeHundred\% at $m=300$) but mostly valid for Wayformer (\agentCorWfForty\% at $m=40$,
\agentCorWfThreeHundred\% at $m=300$), on identical scenes. The difference tracks the size of the miscalibration: the source threshold misses
\rSrcAutobot\% of AutoBot's evaluation agents against \rSrcWayformer\% of Wayformer's, so scene clustering has more room
to hurt a method that was already more wrong. Scene size alone does not predict agent-level failure.

\subsection{Exploratory models}\label{sec:expl}
Three reduced-budget AV2 models and one reduced-budget nuScenes model (Table~\ref{tab:models}) were analysed before
the confirmatory models existed. Each reproduces the sign of every confirmatory finding: under-coverage on the
target, no help from reweighting, losses concentrated in turns, and agent-level failure under scene clustering. The
magnitudes vary by a factor of three, which is why the claims above rest on the confirmatory models alone.
